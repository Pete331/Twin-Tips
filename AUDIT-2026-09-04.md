## 1. Executive summary — the five that matter most

**1. One postponed game silently corrupts the next round's tipping rules.** A round only gets a ladder snapshot when *every* game in it completes. If one game is postponed, no snapshot is ever written, and the next round is judged against a ladder two rounds old. I demonstrated a tip that is **legal under the correct ladder and rejected under the stale one**. A 24-hour escape hatch then opens tipping anyway, so the app looks normal while enforcing the wrong top 8. Nothing anywhere says so. *(C1)*

**2. Late joiners are charged for rounds before they joined.** `joinedAtRound` is written onto every membership and **never read**. A member who joins a weekly pool at round 4 having tipped rounds 1–3 shows `entries=3` — billed $15 they never agreed to — and can win rounds they weren't in. Measured, not inferred. *(C2)*

**3. A slow Squiggle stalls the tips page.** `POST /api/detailsRound` awaits a live-score refresh that calls Squiggle with **no timeout**. I stubbed a hung upstream: the call was still waiting after 4 seconds with nothing to stop it short of undici's 300-second default. It only fires when a round is live — precisely when Squiggle is busiest. *(R1)*

**4. bcrypt blocks the event loop for ~70ms per call, measured on your machine.** Every login, registration and password change freezes the whole server for that time. bcrypt's own README says "We recommend using async API if you use `bcrypt` on a server." On Render's free shared CPU it will be worse. *(S1)*

**5. The code that decides who wins money has four tests; the odds display has sixty-eight.** Coverage is inverted relative to risk. `services/results.js` is the highest-stakes file in the repo. *(T1)*

**What is genuinely good, and worth saying:** server-side lockout enforcement is correct and I could not beat it. Timezone handling is right — Squiggle's `unixtime` is used as the authoritative instant, so Perth vs Melbourne is a rendering question, not a correctness one. The double-submit race is properly closed by a partial unique index. Authorization on league routes is layered correctly. Session fixation is handled (Passport 0.6+ regenerates on login). Zero dependency advisories on either side. The commentary in this codebase is the best I have read in an app this size — most findings below are gaps in reach, not in care.

## 3. Detailed findings

### Correctness

#### C1 — A postponed game silently degrades the next round's ladder — **Critical**

**What's wrong.** `syncStandingsForCompletedRounds` only captures a snapshot for rounds where every fixture reads `complete === 100` ([seasonSync.js:101](services/seasonSync.js#L101), via `completedRounds` at [:84](services/seasonSync.js#L84)). If one game is postponed, that round never completes, so no snapshot is ever written for it. `getLadderForRound` then falls back to "the most recent ranked snapshot that predates the round" ([standings.js:33](services/standings.js#L33)) — two rounds back — and returns it with no signal that it did.

Meanwhile `ladderReady` would have held tipping shut, except `ladderOverdue` opens it again 24 hours after the round's last bounce ([season.js:246](services/season.js#L246)). So the gate designed to catch this releases into the degraded state.

**Why it matters.** The ladder decides who is in the top 8 and who is in the bottom 10 — the entire premise of the competition. I seeded a round-4 ladder in which Hawthorn climbs to 8th and Gold Coast drops to 10th, then validated the same tip under both:

```
round 3 ladder (the fallback): "Hawthorn is not in the top 8."
round 4 ladder (the truth):    null — accepted
```

A legal tip is rejected, and the mirror case — a team that has dropped out of the top 8 — is accepted. The player is told their pick is invalid and has no way to discover why.

**Recommended fix.** Two parts, in order of value:

1. Make the fallback visible. `getLadderForRound` should return which round it actually used, and `getSeasonState` should surface it so the tips page can say "judged on the round 3 ladder — round 4 is incomplete". Silence is the real defect.
2. Consider snapshotting on "every game that will be played has been played" rather than strict completeness, or allow a snapshot for a round where the only incomplete fixtures are older than the staleness window. MongoDB's ranking is Squiggle's, so a partial round still yields a usable ladder.

#### C2 — Late joiners are charged for rounds before they joined — **High**

**What's wrong.** `joinedAtRound` is written on membership creation ([leagues.js:152](routes/leagues.js#L152) and [:222](routes/leagues.js#L222)) and read in exactly one place — echoed back in the members list at [:378](routes/leagues.js#L378). No scoring path consults it. `eligibleRounds` uses the **league-level** `startRound` ([leagueStandings.js:43](services/leagueStandings.js#L43)), which is the same for every member.

**Why it matters.** It is money. Measured on a seeded league with a member joining at round 4 who had tipped rounds 1–3:

```
expected: entries=0
actual:   entries=3  (charged 15 dollars), winnings=3
```

The season ladder has the same hole: `roundsTipped=3, correctTips=3` for rounds they were not a member for. Both directions are wrong — they are billed for rounds they did not enter, and they can take a pool they were not in.

**Recommended fix.** Filter per member, not per league. In `weeklyStandings` and `seasonLadder`, intersect each member's tips with `round >= membership.joinedAtRound`; in `scoreRound`, exclude members whose `joinedAtRound` is greater than the round being scored. The field is already there and already correct — nothing needs migrating.

---

#### C3 — The season tiebreak rewards tipping fewer rounds — Medium (rules, arguably taste)

`rankSeason` sorts on `correctTips` descending, then `marginError` **ascending** ([leagueStandings.js:53](services/leagueStandings.js#L53)), where `marginError` is a running total. Measured:

```
1. skipped_6   tips=30 margin=260 rounds=16
2. played_all  tips=30 margin=400 rounds=22
```

The player who tipped six fewer rounds finishes ahead on equal correct tips, purely because they accumulated less error. The code comment acknowledges this ("in the same way the spec accepts for a player who simply tipped fewer rounds"), so I am flagging it as a rules decision to make consciously rather than a bug. But it is gameable at the margin: once your correct-tip count is settled, skipping a round can only help your tiebreak.

**Options:** average margin error rather than total; or add `roundsTipped` descending as a tiebreak ahead of margin. Either is a few lines in a function that already has tests.

---

#### C4 — `fixtureDate` fallback can store a server-timezone date — Low (latent)

[seasonSync.js:162](services/seasonSync.js#L162) writes `$set: { ...game, ...(date ? { date } : {}) }`. The spread includes Squiggle's raw `date` — a bare local-time string with no zone. When `fixtureDate` returns `null`, that string is what gets cast, in whatever zone the server runs in. This is the exact bug the comment above it describes having fixed, still reachable on the fallback path. Squiggle always sends `unixtime` today, so it does not fire — but it is one upstream change away.

**Fix:** `const { date: _ignored, ...rest } = game;` before the spread, and write `date` only from `fixtureDate`.

---

#### C5 — Consecutive-round rule assumes contiguous numbering — Low

The "can't pick the same team two rounds running" check loads `round - 1` ([api-routes.js:279](routes/api-routes.js#L279)). If a season ever has a gap in round numbers, the previous tip is simply not found and the rule quietly stops applying. Use "the greatest round less than this one that has fixtures".

### Security

#### S1 — Synchronous bcrypt blocks the event loop — **High**

**What's wrong.** `bcrypt.hashSync`, `genSaltSync` and `compareSync` are used throughout [authController.js](controllers/authController.js) (lines 128, 212, 216, 316) and [passport.js:46](config/passport.js#L46).

**Measured on your machine:**

```
hashSync (cost 10)      71.1 ms   event loop blocked
compareSync (match)     68.4 ms   event loop blocked
await compare()         70.4 ms wall, 0 ms blocked
```

**Why it matters.** Node is single-threaded. Every login freezes *all* other requests for ~70ms. Render's free tier is shared CPU, so expect several times that. Twenty people signing in when the round drops is a visible stall for everyone else — and the login limiter allows 20 attempts per IP per 15 minutes, so it is also a cheap way to degrade the service.

**Fix, from the library's own README** ([node.bcrypt.js](https://github.com/kelektiv/node.bcrypt.js)): *"Bcrypt hashing is CPU intensive which will cause the sync APIs to block the event loop and prevent your application from servicing any inbound requests or events. We recommend using async API if you use bcrypt on a server."* All five call sites are already inside `async` functions or promise chains — `await bcrypt.hash(password, 10)` and `await bcrypt.compare(...)` drop straight in. The comments explaining why sync was chosen describe a callback-throw bug that `await` also solves.

---

#### S2 — Password change and reset leave other sessions valid — Medium

`changePassword` and `resetPassword` update the hash and nothing else. Any other signed-in device keeps its session for the full 30-day rolling window. The main reason to reset a password is to remove someone else's access, and this does not.

**Fix:** sessions are in Mongo via connect-mongo. After a successful change, delete this user's other sessions — `db.collection("sessions").deleteMany({ "session.passport.user": String(userId) })`, excluding the current `req.sessionID`.

---

#### S3, S4, S5, S6 — Low

- **S3** `GET /api/auth/logout` ([routes/api/auth/index.js:33](routes/api/auth/index.js#L33)) changes state on a GET. `sameSite: lax` blocks subresource requests, so the practical exposure is a link click, but it should be a POST.
- **S4** `/api/auth/reset` has no limiter while `/forgot` has one. The token is 40 CSPRNG bytes so brute force is not the concern; a limiter is one line and closes the door anyway.
- **S5** `findByIdentifier` returns early for an unknown identifier without doing a bcrypt compare ([passport.js:42](config/passport.js#L42)), so a missing account answers ~70ms faster than a wrong password — enough to enumerate accounts. Compare against a dummy hash on the miss path.
- **S6** Registration checks email uniqueness with `findOne` then saves. Two simultaneous registrations both pass and the second fails on the unique index, surfacing as "Internal server issue!". Catch code `11000` and return the friendly message.

**Checked and clean:** no secrets in tracked files (`.env` is gitignored; the `xkeysib-` hit is documentation in `.env.example`). No CORS misconfiguration — the app is same-origin by construction. Helmet CSP is tight and `connectSrc: 'self'` genuinely holds. The Squiggle proxy is allowlisted by query type *and* parameter shape, so it is not an SSRF relay. Session fixation is handled by Passport 0.6+, which regenerates the session on login and logout ([Passport docs](https://www.passportjs.org/concepts/authentication/sessions/)).

**Still outstanding from earlier work:** the Atlas password pasted into chat in an earlier session has not been rotated.

### Reliability

#### R1 — No timeout on Squiggle, awaited in the request path — **High**

**What's wrong.** [services/squiggle.js:34](services/squiggle.js#L34) calls `fetch(url, { headers })` with no `signal`. Node's fetch has no overall request timeout; it falls back to undici's 300-second header timeout.

**Why it matters.** [api-routes.js:127](routes/api-routes.js#L127) — the tips page fixture load — does `await liveScores.refreshIfLive(...)`, which calls Squiggle. `refreshIfLive` catches errors, but catching is not cancelling. Demonstrated with a stubbed hung upstream:

```
after 4015ms: still waiting
verdict: no timeout, the caller waits on undici's 300s default
```

This path only runs when a round is live — the exact window when Squiggle is under load and when your users are all on the page.

**Fix:** `fetch(url, { headers, signal: AbortSignal.timeout(5000) })`. One line, in one place, since every Squiggle call goes through this client. Consider a shorter budget for the request-path caller than for the cron.

### Data layer

#### D1 — Four hot reads are collection scans — Medium

The only non-`_id` index on `tips` is `{user, round, season}` (unique, partial). Queries that filter by round and season without a user cannot use it. Measured with `explain("executionStats")` on your data:

| Query | Plan | Docs examined | Returned |
|---|---|---|---|
| `POST /api/roundResult` | **COLLSCAN** | 135 | 6 |
| `POST /api/leaderboard` | **COLLSCAN** | 135 | 126 |
| `results.calculateRound` | **COLLSCAN** | 135 | 6 |
| league standings tips join | **COLLSCAN** | 135 | 15 |
| `getSeasonState` fixtures | IXSCAN | 218 | 218 |
| `getLadderForRound` | IXSCAN | 18 | 18 |
| `Team` by Squiggle id | **COLLSCAN** | 18 | 1 |

At 135 documents this costs nothing. It scales with *every tip ever submitted*: 100 players × 24 rounds × 5 seasons is 12,000 documents scanned on every home page load, and the hourly cron does it once per round.

**Fix:** `tipSchema.index({ season: 1, round: 1 })` and `teamSchema.index({ id: 1 })`. Both are additive and safe to build online.

#### D3 — No schema-level validation on `Tips` — Low

Every field is optional with no validator. All correctness lives in the route. That is defensible while there is one writer, but the partial unique index already had to be written around junk 2022 documents with no `user` and no `season` — evidence of what happens when the schema does not hold the line. Adding `required` to `user`, `round`, `season` costs nothing and would have prevented those.

### Performance

- **P1 — `getSeasonState` is the app's hottest path and does the most work.** Two queries (`resolveSeason` then `Fixture.find({year})`), loading every fixture of the season (~207 documents) and sorting in memory, on every call — and `SeasonContext` calls it on every page load. Nothing is cached. A 30–60 second in-process cache keyed on season would remove almost all of it; the data changes hourly at most.
- **P2 — `deserializeUser` does a `User.findById` on every authenticated request** ([passport.js:63](config/passport.js#L63)). Conventional, but it is a database round trip per request on a cold-starting free instance. The session already holds enough to skip it for most routes.
- **P3 — `moment` costs 60.19 kB (19.61 kB gzip) for four calls.** The project's own documentation says: *"Moment.js is a legacy project in maintenance mode"* and it does not recommend it for new projects, naming Luxon, Day.js, date-fns and js-Joda as alternatives ([project status](https://momentjs.com/docs/#/-project-status/)). Four call sites is a small migration for a meaningful chunk of the bundle.
- **P4 — Eight `@fontsource/roboto` imports** ([App/index.jsx:11–18](client/src/components/App/index.jsx#L11)) produce 16 font files in the build (~280 kB). Four weights × two subsets. Most apps need two weights, and `latin-ext` is only needed for accented characters your team names do not use.
- **P5 — The hourly cron re-scores every completed round of the season, every hour.** `calculateSeason` loops all rounds and re-runs `calculateRound` on each ([results.js:200](services/results.js#L200)). It is idempotent, which is why it is safe — but Render bills the cron by running time, and by September that is 24 rounds of redundant work every hour. Score only rounds that completed since the last run.

**Already handled well:** the static-asset cache policy in `server.js` is unusually careful, distinguishing content-hashed files from hand-named ones. The client has route-level code splitting. The 45-second axios timeout is correctly sized for a Render cold start.

## 6. UI/UX assessment and competitive research

### My assessment of what you have

**Strong.** The information density on the round results table is right — player, both tips, and the score in one row, with tick/cross carrying correctness in shape as well as colour. Contrast is measurably good (I measured the real palette; every text pair clears WCAG AA, most by a wide margin — see below). The bottom navigation on mobile is the correct pattern for three destinations. Skeleton loaders exist and are used. The league picker doubling as the create/join entry point is the right call.

**Measured contrast, on the palette the app actually uses:**

| Pair | Ratio | |
|---|---|---|
| Body text on white card | 16.10:1 | AA |
| Body text on page grey | 14.76:1 | AA |
| Text on green/red result tints | 14.3 / 14.1:1 | AA |
| White on navbar navy | 10.32:1 | AA |
| Secondary text on white | 4.83:1 | AA |
| Link / action blue on white | 4.60:1 | AA |
| MUI disabled text | 2.68:1 | Fails, but disabled controls are exempt under WCAG |

**Weak.**

1. **Nothing happens if you forget.** Miss a round and you get zero, silently, with no recovery. This is the single biggest gap versus every competitor (below).
2. **The tipping flow is not one-handed.** Two selections plus a numeric margin field, with checkboxes on cards spread down a scrolling page. Getting tips in requires scrolling, two taps at opposite ends of the viewport, and a keyboard for the margin.
3. **No sense of "what happened while I was away."** Opening the app after a round tells you the standings but not the story — who won, whether you moved, what it cost you.
4. **Empty states are thin.** A new user with no league sees a global ladder of strangers. Better than nothing, but it does not explain what the competition *is*.
5. **The `*` on a fixture card still has no legend** (the round table now has one).

### What the competition does better

**Superbru — the default pick.** If you miss a pick, Superbru assigns "the team the community most expects to win", and *"You are eligible for 7 default picks in AFL Tipping"* ([scoring rules](https://old.superbru.com/afltipping/how_to_play_scoring.php)). It solves the retention problem directly: missing a round does not eliminate you, and the allowance is capped so it cannot be abused. It also gives them a natural push notification ("we tipped for you — change it before Thursday?").

**Official AFL Tipping — the "Forgot to Tip" preference.** Set at registration and editable later, it auto-tips on your behalf when you miss; combined with push notifications so "you never forget to tip" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/official-afl-tipping/id1454632484), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fanhubmedia.afltipping)). Note that the same listings carry complaints that advertising covers the bottom menu — a reminder that the official app is not a UX model to copy wholesale, only to steal specific mechanics from.

**Superbru's shared bonus point.** Their BP goes to the closest margin in your pool and is *shared, down to a minimum of 0.25* — a nice precedent for your own pool-splitting, which already does exact fractional division.

### What I would steal, in order

1. **A default tip.** Twin Tips has a natural one that no competitor can offer: your top-8 and bottom-10 picks are constrained to two teams you have not used last round, and you already compute the ladder. A "tip for me" fallback — highest-ranked eligible top-8 side and lowest-ranked eligible bottom-10 side, with a default margin — is a handful of lines in the scoring path and turns a missed round from elimination into a weak entry. Cap it, as Superbru does.
2. **A pre-lockout reminder.** You have already scoped this. The default tip makes the reminder far more valuable: "we've tipped Geelong and North Melbourne for you — tap to change before 6:10pm."
3. **A one-screen tip entry for mobile.** A sticky summary bar showing both picks and the margin, with the submit button inside thumb reach, so the page can scroll without losing the state of the tip.
4. **A "what happened" card on return.** One line at the top of Home after a round settles: where you finished, who won the round, and what it did to your balance.
5. **A real empty state for a new user.** Explain the format — pick one from the top 8, one from the bottom 10, nominate one margin — before showing them a ladder.

### Accessibility

Contrast is fine. The gaps are elsewhere: the tipping checkboxes carry no accessible label beyond the team name, the margin inputs use `inputProps` (deprecated in MUI v9 in favour of `slotProps.htmlInput`) and are labelled only "Margin" with no association to which game, and there is no skip link. None are hard; none are contrast problems, which is where people usually look first.
